## 3. Publishing mix

Each normal week contains two new-content slots and five optimization/maintenance slots:

- **1 utility or evergreen slot:** usually OhCrisp storage, moisture, texture, rehydration, or practical use; two of these slots become the guide pages.
- **1 differentiated brand/editorial slot:** alternate Frozili and sourced first-party snack culture. Do not publish generic “product for occasion X” variations.
- **2 high-impression refresh slots:** rewrite title/description only when the page answer and search intent also improve.
- **1 internal-link/cluster slot.**
- **1 technical/indexing slot.**
- **1 GSC review and next-week briefing slot.**

No article is commissioned until its primary intent has been checked against existing titles, slugs, and Search Console queries. A new URL must answer a materially different question; otherwise improve the existing URL. The current evidence does not support daily publishing.

## 5. Old-content decision rules

Every reviewed URL receives one decision:

- **Keep:** useful, distinct, and accurate; only repair links or metadata if needed.
- **Refresh:** search intent is sound but the answer, examples, evidence, or structure is weak.
- **Consolidate:** two pages answer essentially the same question. Move the best material into the stronger URL and 301 redirect the weaker one.
- **Retire/noindex:** only when content is inaccurate, unhelpful, has no meaningful replacement, and has no valuable links or traffic.

For a substantive refresh:

- improve the answer, not just the wording;
- add first-hand sensory/product detail where available;
- add 2–4 relevant internal links;
- update `dateModified` only after a real content change;
- keep FAQs only when they represent genuine follow-up questions;
- rebuild and validate the generated page.

## 9. Daily operating checklist

### Before writing

1. Check the proposed intent against every existing slug/title and the top Search Console queries.
2. Write one sentence explaining what new value the page adds.
3. Confirm any factual or trend claims with primary/reliable sources.

### Before publishing

1. The first two paragraphs provide a direct answer or useful premise.
2. The post contains a concrete observation, test, comparison, or genuinely useful technique.
3. Internal links are descriptive and point to the correct clean URLs.
4. Visible content and structured data match.
5. Run the build, static SEO and cover validators, applicable tests and deployment dry-run, then affected mobile/desktop browser checks. Review and isolate the exact release source before automatic commit, `git push origin main` and deployment to the existing site.

### After publishing

1. Confirm the clean URL and canonical.
2. Confirm it is present in the regenerated sitemap.
3. Record the URL, target intent, publish/refresh type, and related cluster in the monthly log.
4. Propose recrawl requests only for the most important new guides and substantial flagship refreshes; GSC writes require separate approval, and small edits do not need manual submission.

## 10. Measurement

Track the new cohort, refreshed cohort, and untouched control pages separately.

Weekly:

- indexed URLs and crawl issues;
- clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position by page/query;
- canonical-origin share and remaining impressions on `www`, `http`, `.html`, and `post.html` variants;
- `/a/` cohort CTR versus the 0.62% starting baseline;
- pages with overlapping queries;
- internal-link coverage and build errors;
- brand-page clicks from Journal articles.

At day 30:

- production: new, refreshed, merged, and validated counts;
- early search signals: impressions and number of ranking queries;
- content quality: percentage with a unique observation/test and distinct intent;
- conversion proxy: clicks from Journal to Frozili/OhCrisp pages.

Do not declare success or failure from a few days of ranking movement. Use the month to improve the content system; assess the cohort again 4–8 weeks after recrawl.
